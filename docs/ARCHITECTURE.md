# Architecture and tuning

## Match

`src/match.ts` owns scores, elapsed ticks, tactics, player controllers, ball physics and the bounded match state. `src/football.ts` holds units, pitch dimensions, roster, randomness and the swept goal test. The ball uses a Rapier dynamic sphere with gravity, damping, rolling resistance and CCD. Player capsules use position-based kinematic bodies. The goal line, post, board and keeper reach use pitch coordinates shared with the stadium. The simulation and leg rig share a reachable ankle target and contact time. The ball receives its impulse and spin when the boot reaches it. The supporting foot plants for the strike. Keepers cradle catches in live play. During a throw restart the assigned player carries the ball to the release spot.

Possession depends on measured distance, speed, height and temporary action cooldowns. Each team assigns one primary outfield challenger and allocates the remaining players to defensive cover, receiving or attack lanes. Ball carriers compare shot opening and range, lane clearance, opponent pressure, support movement and how long they have held possession. Passes lead the recipient and offer a return run. Aerial passes check for space on takeoff and at the receiving end. The kick uses the release position and the receiving player tracks the actual descending ball. All match decisions use one seeded random stream. Render and audio effects have independent seeded streams.

`src/goalkeeping.ts` estimates which incoming shots threaten the goalmouth and classifies the reachable shot by height and lateral distance. Each keeper shifts to cover the near post and sets their feet as a threat approaches. Reach and ball speed affect the save. The save event records the move and whether the keeper held the ball or parried it wide. A catch still leads to a timed distribution.

Goals use live → goal → kickoff → live. The first outward crossing by the whole ball settles the ruling. A goal advances the score and goal ID once. The same dynamic ball continues against the back, side and roof of the net. The first mesh contact sets the location of its ripple. The 330-tick goal presentation and 37-tick kickoff fit within the running match clock. A period whistle can end the celebration early.

A sideline, wide or high exit uses live → throw-in → live. The ball coasts to the apron and its last touch determines the receiving team. A keeper or outfielder runs to the ball, carries it to the proper spot, lifts it, and launches a Rapier flight to a teammate. The receiver can be challenged on arrival. Fault and dead-ball recovery handle invalid coordinates or a stalled ball.

The series clock reaches halftime at tick 36,000 of each fixture and full time at tick 72,000. Halftime retires the ball and has the players run to the other end before the second-half kickoff. At full time the previous score and statistics become a report, the win/draw tally advances, and the next fixture clock starts at 00:00. The 600-tick celebration and 37-tick kickoff setup fit inside that schedule. Goal attribution follows the current attack direction, and opening kickoff alternates by fixture. The session clock keeps running across fixtures for deterministic events, lighting, and effect timing.

## Presentation

`src/main.ts` owns the fixed-step accumulator, HUD, checkpoints, operator controls and diagnostics. It caps catch-up at 15 fixed steps per frame. `src/renderer.ts` interpolates the scene and adapts drawing-buffer size and crowd/flag/effect update cost. Resizes and supported WebGL context restoration reuse scene data. `src/stadium.ts` makes the arena, procedural turf, instanced spectators, moving flags and flexing goal nets. `src/characters.ts` builds the articulated players with a shared instancing pass. Limb motion follows displacement, and kick impulses use the same contact timing constant as the pose. `src/audio.ts` synthesizes locally, compresses the mix and caps short sound events at ten concurrent voices.

`src/show.ts` assigns names, schedules upper-body reactions from real events, remembers recent rivalries, and ranks plays for the next break. Boulder has an orange headband and bows when he scores. Zip wears a mint scarf and finishes with a wink and a flourish. Save, post, and steal jokes have individual cooldowns to keep them from crowding one another out. `src/characters.ts` layers the performance poses over live locomotion. Match movement and collision rules are shared.

`src/camera.ts` follows the rendered ball and players from a low sideline angle. It fits the current subjects against aspect-specific screen margins. Nearby passers, receivers and challengers get a lower two-player angle, long balls lead toward the landing area, and the goal camera shows the crossing, then the scorer and their supporters. Translation keeps the ball in the live picture as the lens eases wider. The football casts a soft contact shadow whose size and opacity follow its height. `src/broadcast-layout.ts` uses projected screen positions to keep floating cards clear of the ball and featured faces. A rotating player spotlight appears at a kickoff or goalkeeper distribution and draws on actual match events.

`src/arena-show.ts` turns match events into timed stadium reactions. It keeps a goal on the boards through its celebration and uses the previous fixture's score at the final whistle. `src/arena-decor.ts` paints the screen and pitch-side boards when the cue changes. `src/arena-crowd.ts` gives fans one of eight illustrated animal faces, a team kit and articulated cheering arms. The stadium also animates two concourse mascots outside the physics world. Saves and goals trigger a short clap on the effects channel. The stereo music bus lowers during speech and feeds the same compressor and capture output as the booth.

`src/surroundings.ts` extends the ground under the arena and builds the paths, planted entrances, neighborhood, and hills outside it. The sky has a sun and two drifting cloud layers. Cloud color, window light, and fog follow the stadium's daylight cycle. Buildings and plants are instanced and stay outside the match physics. Both recording orientations use a lower opening angle to show the landscape and then move in for play.

Keepers project incoming flight to the goal and to their glove plane. A committed dive or jump has a loading phase, ballistic vertical travel, and a braking slide on landing. The same two glove positions drive the articulated arms and the swept contact test. Rapier handles capsule blocks. Holding a shot depends on its speed and the second glove's reach. A parry uses incoming velocity and glove motion. The gloves cradle the physical ball after a catch and let it fall before a distribution kick. Save captions and effects use the result of contact.

A push pass carries forward roll. Lofted passes and punts carry backspin. Shots include aiming error from distance, angle and nearby pressure. Airborne spin contributes a bounded Magnus force, and a lofted pass accounts for lift when targeting its receiver.

`src/heroes.ts` defines ten player identities shared by the on-field models, portraits, hero picker, and spotlights. The face profile sets each player's outline, muzzle, eyes, ears, and markings in both the 3D model and the 2D portrait. A browser-local favorite can be selected or cleared in the picker. The selected player's gold ring follows their feet. The match runs while the picker is open.

Drive, loft, switch, and long-shot events carry a destination and expected travel time. The camera widens around the ball and destination before returning to the live formation. The mixer uses a different sound for a lofted pass and a long shot.

During the goal break, the camera holds on the net for 86 ticks, follows the credited scorer, then shows the scoring supporters. The scoring side's captain leads on an own goal or when no scorer is recorded. Confetti bursts follow the leader, and the mixer plays the team's goal jingle. The full-time camera frames the winner before both teams wave. On a draw both sides wave throughout. The camera returns to midfield as soon as the ball is placed for kickoff.

`src/replay.ts` samples the live render into a fixed ring of 18 small frames and stores the selected play in a canvas atlas. A two-second inset plays during the half and match reports. The report can still describe the play if too few frames were available. Highlight ranking considers saves, hits off the post, and steals along with goals. Highlights accumulate anew after a reload.

Capture mode uses the same match simulation with a different layout and camera. `src/production.ts` records fixture ticks and elapsed take time separately, retains the final fixture score at the reset, and writes captions and completed exchanges. `scripts/record-take.mjs` records video from the local tab and audio from the game mixer. `scripts/edit-episode.mjs` selects the cut from recorded events, keeps scoring plays in order, and retimes subtitles to the resulting MP4.

The band director in `src/music-program.ts` chooses four eight-bar movements per visit to a tune. Each return visits the other four arrangements. `src/stadium-band.ts` schedules both recorded parts on the audio clock with their release tails intact. Pass sequences, shots and close finishes bring up a second ensemble. Goal, save and period cues enter on the current tune’s half beat. The director preloads the next movement, releases older buffers and keeps time independently of the match random stream. Explicit game seeds reproduce the programme for a take. A fresh unseeded broadcast draws a new programme.

`src/commentary.ts` selects from 480 recorded calls by two booth hosts. It rotates event variants, schedules each joke’s authored answer after its setup, and clears pending replies on a goal or stoppage. Scorer, keeper technique, kick style, score and match phase determine the eligible lines. `src/commentary-booth.ts` draws Jom and Jerry in the commentary room. Mouth movements follow the clip waveform, including between words. Speech shares a single mixer channel that ducks music and effects. The browser decodes the local PCM bank at its native 24 kHz. Capture logs identify the host and retain the player IDs from the play that prompted a booth exchange.

Low, medium and high profiles change visual resources only. Auto starts at high, steps down after sustained over-budget frames, and waits longer before stepping back up. Contact shadows are flat soft silhouettes. The broadcast camera follows the action with the same left-to-right orientation throughout the match. The debug-only camera selector is used by the screenshots and runs alongside live play.

## Recovery and tuning

One versioned checkpoint is written every 15 simulated seconds, after goals, at halftime and full time, and when the page hides or exits. It contains roster identity, display names, seed and random state, clock, current score and statistics, series tally, goal ID, effective restart side, and preferences. Normal page loads and refreshes start a fresh fixture. A URL with `session=resume` can restore a matching checkpoint and reconstruct the players and ball for a safe kickoff. Saved preferences carry forward on every load. The event log keeps at most 96 entries. Net and spark effects reuse bounded objects, the sound mixer caps concurrent voices, and the match uses 11 bodies and 36 colliders throughout.

Tune pressing and support in `tactics`, pass/shot choice in `chooseKick`, and keeper timing in `keeperTarget` and `findTouch`. After tuning, rerun `npm test` and `npm run test:stress`. Check each seed's shot share, saves, goals, recovery reasons and longest interval between goals. Use production browser captures to validate how changes read from the broadcast camera.
