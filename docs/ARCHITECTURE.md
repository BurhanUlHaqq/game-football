# Architecture and tuning

## Match

`src/match.ts` owns scores, elapsed ticks, tactics, player controllers, ball physics and the bounded match state. `src/football.ts` holds units, pitch dimensions, roster, randomness and the swept goal test. The ball uses a Rapier dynamic sphere with gravity, damping, rolling resistance and CCD. Player capsules use position-based kinematic bodies. The goal line, post, board and keeper reach use pitch coordinates shared with the stadium. Animated feet apply contact through a small control window. Kicks use impulses and brief player-specific collision exclusions. Only a keeper holding a catch can carry the ball in their hands.

Possession depends on measured distance, speed, height and temporary action cooldowns. Each team assigns one primary outfield challenger and allocates the remaining players to defensive cover, receiving or attack lanes. Ball carriers compare shot opening and range, lane clearance, opponent pressure, support movement and how long they have held possession. Passes lead the recipient and offer a return run. Aerial passes check for space on takeoff and at the receiving end. The kick uses the release position and the receiving player tracks the actual descending ball. All match decisions use one seeded random stream. Render and audio effects have independent seeded streams.

`src/goalkeeping.ts` estimates which incoming shots threaten the goalmouth and classifies the reachable shot by height and lateral distance. Each keeper shifts to cover the near post and sets their feet as a threat approaches. Reach and ball speed affect the save. The save event records the move and whether the keeper held the ball or parried it wide. A catch still leads to a timed distribution.

The normal state path is setup → kickoff → live. Goals use live → goal → kickoff → live. Escapes, stalls and invalid coordinates take bounded retrieval or fault states through kickoff. A goal changes the defending net’s opponent score once, advances the goal ID and disables the one ball body until the next kickoff. The four-second goal celebration and the 37-tick kickoff setup take 277 fixed steps before live play resumes. A scheduled whistle can cut the goal presentation short. Own goals use the same net-based attribution, with a separate own-goal presentation. Physics coordinate corruption is repaired in world, and recurring faults appear in diagnostics and the broadcast console.

The series clock reaches halftime at tick 36,000 of each fixture and full time at tick 72,000. Halftime retires the ball and has the players run to the other end before the second-half kickoff. At full time the previous score and statistics become a report, the win/draw tally advances, and the next fixture clock starts at 00:00. The 600-tick celebration and 37-tick kickoff setup fit inside that schedule. Goal attribution follows the current attack direction, and opening kickoff alternates by fixture. The session clock keeps running across fixtures for deterministic events, lighting, and effect timing.

## Presentation

`src/main.ts` owns the fixed-step accumulator, HUD, checkpoints, operator controls and diagnostics. It caps catch-up at 15 fixed steps per frame. `src/renderer.ts` interpolates the scene and adapts drawing-buffer size and crowd/flag/effect update cost. Resizes and supported WebGL context restoration reuse scene data. `src/stadium.ts` makes the arena, procedural turf, instanced spectators, moving flags and flexing goal nets. `src/characters.ts` builds the articulated players with a shared instancing pass. Limb motion follows displacement, and kick impulses use the same contact timing constant as the pose. `src/audio.ts` synthesizes locally, compresses the mix and caps short sound events at ten concurrent voices.

`src/show.ts` assigns names, schedules upper-body reactions from real events, remembers recent rivalries, and ranks plays for the next break. Boulder has an orange headband and bows when he scores. Zip wears a mint scarf and finishes with a wink and a flourish. Save, post, and steal jokes have individual cooldowns to keep them from crowding one another out. `src/characters.ts` layers the performance poses over live locomotion. Match movement and collision rules are shared.

`src/camera.ts` groups the ball, shooter and defending keeper in attack and can frame both players in a steal. The renderer draws a ground locator and short impact cue. `src/broadcast-layout.ts` uses projected screen positions to keep floating cards clear of the ball and featured players. A caption can include the event label. A rotating player spotlight appears at a kickoff or goalkeeper distribution and draws on actual match events.

`src/arena-show.ts` turns match events into timed stadium reactions. It keeps a goal on the boards through its celebration and uses the previous fixture's score at the final whistle. `src/arena-decor.ts` paints the screen and pitch-side boards when the cue changes. `src/arena-crowd.ts` gives each seat a stable combination of figure, clothes, and headwear and animates instanced fans and pennants by section. The stadium also animates two concourse mascots outside the physics world. The same cue changes crowd volume on the effects channel, which still ducks beneath dialogue.

`src/surroundings.ts` extends the ground under the arena and builds the paths, planted entrances, neighborhood, and hills outside it. The sky has a sun and two drifting cloud layers. Cloud color, window light, and fog follow the stadium's daylight cycle. Buildings and plants are instanced and stay outside the match physics. Both recording orientations use a lower opening angle to show the landscape and then move in for play.

The goalkeepers have a set stance, a lateral shuffle, and separate poses for low scoops, central blocks, side dives, and high tips. The renderer positions the visible ball between the gloves while the keeper holds it. The match still uses the live ball's physics position for release and play. Save captions and effects use the move and outcome recorded by the simulation.

`src/heroes.ts` defines ten player identities shared by the on-field models, portraits, hero picker, and spotlights. The face profile sets each player's outline, muzzle, eyes, ears, and markings in both the 3D model and the 2D portrait. A browser-local favorite can be selected or cleared in the picker. The renderer reuses the locator geometry for the favorite's gold ring. The match runs while the picker is open.

Drive, loft, switch, and long-shot events carry a destination and expected travel time. The camera widens around the ball and destination before returning to the live formation. The locator brightens beneath a higher kick. The mixer uses a different sound for a lofted pass and a long shot.

During the goal break, the scoring team cycles through its routines and the camera follows the credited scorer. The scoring side's captain leads on an own goal or when no scorer is recorded. Confetti bursts follow the leader, and the mixer plays the team's goal jingle. The full-time camera frames the winner before both teams wave. On a draw both sides wave throughout. The camera returns to midfield as soon as the ball is placed for kickoff.

`src/replay.ts` samples the live render into a fixed ring of 18 small frames and stores the selected play in a canvas atlas. A two-second inset plays during the half and match reports. The report can still describe the play if too few frames were available. Highlight ranking considers saves, hits off the post, and steals along with goals. Highlights accumulate anew after a reload.

Capture mode uses the same match simulation with a different layout and camera. `src/production.ts` records fixture ticks and elapsed take time separately, retains the final fixture score at the reset, and writes captions and completed exchanges. `scripts/record-take.mjs` records video from the local tab and audio from the game mixer. `scripts/edit-episode.mjs` selects the cut from recorded events, keeps scoring plays in order, and retimes subtitles to the resulting MP4.

`src/commentary.ts` uses 365 prerecorded lines and their clip durations to space twelve voices. It rotates to the least recently played variation for each event or player turn. Commentary can refer to the scorer, a specific keeper move, a long-range shot, or a switch of play when the simulation records it. Speech shares one channel that ducks music and effects, and the PCM bank is decoded at its native 24 kHz. A pass gives its actual recipient a chance to reply. A save, tackle, or interception can produce an exchange between the two players involved. The scheduler spaces exchanges and favors players who have spoken less. Goals and match whistles take priority. Players use head and mouth motion during their exchanges, and captions identify both participants even when speech is muted or blocked.

Low, medium and high profiles change visual resources only. Auto starts at high, steps down after sustained over-budget frames, and waits longer before stepping back up. Contact shadows are flat soft silhouettes. The broadcast camera follows the action with the same left-to-right orientation throughout the match. The debug-only camera selector is used by the screenshots and runs alongside live play.

## Recovery and tuning

One versioned checkpoint is written every 15 simulated seconds, after goals, at halftime and full time, and when the page hides or exits. It contains roster identity, display names, seed and random state, clock, current score and statistics, series tally, goal ID, effective restart side, and preferences. Normal page loads and refreshes start a fresh fixture. A URL with `session=resume` can restore a matching checkpoint and reconstruct the players and ball for a safe kickoff. Saved preferences carry forward on every load. The event log keeps at most 96 entries. Net and spark effects reuse bounded objects, the sound mixer caps concurrent voices, and the match uses 11 bodies and 30 colliders throughout.

Tune pressing and support in `tactics`, pass/shot choice in `chooseKick`, and keeper timing in `keeperTarget` and `findTouch`. After tuning, rerun `npm test` and `npm run test:stress`. Check each seed's shot share, saves, goals, recovery reasons and longest interval between goals. Use production browser captures to validate how changes read from the broadcast camera.
